Cranes and Rigging

Crane work depends on a trained operator, a qualified rigger and a clear signal person working together. The biggest risks are power line contact, dropped loads and overloading the crane.

Training required

  • Crane operators must be certified or qualified (OSHA 1926.1427)
  • Riggers must be qualified to rig the load (OSHA 1926.1425)
  • Signal persons must be qualified and know the standard hand signals (OSHA 1926.1428)
  • Workers near the crane must be trained on swing radius and power line hazards

Before you lift

  • Check the load chart and confirm the weight of the load
  • Inspect slings, hooks, shackles and other rigging hardware for damage
  • Set up on firm, level ground with outriggers fully extended as required
  • Stay at least 20 feet from power lines up to 350 kV, or follow the encroachment plan
  • Agree on hand signals with one designated signal person
  • Never stand or walk under a suspended load

Official instructions and resources

  • OSHA cranes and derricks standard (29 CFR 1926 Subpart CC)
  • OSHA cranes and derricks FAQ
