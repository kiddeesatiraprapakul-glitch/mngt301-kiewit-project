Aerial and Scissor Lifts

Boom lifts and scissor lifts put workers at height, so falls, tip-overs and being caught against overhead objects are the main hazards.

Training required

  • Only trained and authorized workers may operate an aerial lift (OSHA 1926.453)
  • Scissor lift users are trained as scaffold users (OSHA 1926.454)
  • Training covers fall protection, load limits, electrical hazards and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Retrain when equipment changes or a worker shows they need it

Before you go up

  • Test the lift controls before each shift
  • Check the ground for holes, drop-offs, slopes and soft spots
  • Look up for power lines, beams and other overhead hazards
  • Boom lifts: wear a harness with the lanyard tied off to the basket
  • Scissor lifts: keep guardrails and gates closed and stand on the platform floor
  • Never exceed the rated platform capacity or use the lift as a crane

Official instructions and resources

  • OSHA aerial lifts standard (29 CFR 1926.453)
  • OSHA scaffold training requirements (29 CFR 1926.454)

Always follow the manufacturer's instructions, your company's procedures and the full OSHA standard. If you are unsure, stop and ask your foreman or safety professional.
